# Private Brand-Rename Discussion

## The respectful case for looking now

The original **J9's Canni-Bombs** name belongs to the product's first chapter. It is personal, playful, and connected to the friend who made the original logo. None of that should be dismissed.

The relaunch creates a rare low-cost moment to compare that heritage with a name designed for the next decade. The brand does not yet have meaningful search equity, retail recognition, printed packaging inventory, or a large customer base that would be disrupted by a change.

The current name has several commercial limitations:

- The numeral, apostrophe, hyphen, and plural make it visually busy and easier to mistype.
- “Canni-Bombs” leads with cannabis novelty before craftsmanship, fragrance, gifting, and ritual.
- It sounds playful and product-specific rather than boutique and expandable.
- Future bath salts, oils, balms, scrubs, candles, and gift boxes would sit awkwardly beneath a bath-bomb-only identity.
- The tone is closer to a novelty counter product than a $20 handmade luxury ritual.

This is not an argument that the original idea was wrong. It is an invitation to give the product a name that matches what it has grown into.

## Strongest naming directions

These are creative directions only. Trademark, domain, and social-handle clearance must happen before adoption.

### 1. Private Hour

**Position:** The bath as protected personal time.

**Why it works:** Elegant, emotional, memorable, gender-inclusive, and expandable across many self-care products. It supports lines such as “The hour belongs to you” and “Enter your Private Hour.”

**Potential weakness:** It does not immediately explain that the product is botanical or cannabinoid-infused; packaging and subcopy must do that work.

**Descriptor:** `Private Hour — Botanical Bath Rituals`

### 2. Soft Ending

**Position:** A better way to finish a hard day.

**Why it works:** Poetic, distinctive, warm, and closely tied to the strongest copy direction. It naturally supports the service-worker and after-work audience without sounding occupational.

**Potential weakness:** Slightly abstract; the visual identity must make the bath category obvious.

**Descriptor:** `Soft Ending — Handmade Bath & Body`

### 3. Salt & Bloom

**Position:** Mineral ritual meets botanical beauty.

**Why it works:** Immediately feels like premium bath and body. It is giftable, visually rich, and supports florals, herbs, salts, oils, and future product expansion.

**Potential weakness:** This naming construction is popular in beauty and hospitality, so legal/domain clearance may be difficult.

**Descriptor:** `Salt & Bloom — Cannabinoid-Infused Bath Rituals`

### 4. Ninefold Rituals

**Position:** A refined evolution that quietly preserves the J9 history.

**Why it works:** Keeps “nine” as an emotional bridge without the juvenile look of the numeral/apostrophe construction. “Rituals” allows expansion beyond bath bombs.

**Potential weakness:** The story behind “nine” will not be obvious unless the founder story explains it.

**Descriptor:** `Ninefold Rituals — Made by Jenine`

### 5. After Hours Bath Co.

**Position:** The product for when work, responsibility, and availability end.

**Why it works:** Strong commercial clarity for hairstylists, nurses, bartenders, hospitality workers, parents, and anyone on their feet all day. Easy campaign language and creator content.

**Potential weakness:** More lifestyle-forward and less romantic than Private Hour or Salt & Bloom.

**Descriptor:** `After Hours — Bath Rituals for Off-the-Clock Bodies`

## Recommendation

**Private Hour** is the strongest long-term luxury brand.

**Ninefold Rituals** is the strongest compromise if preserving emotional continuity matters most.

**Soft Ending** is the most distinctive expression of the brand's emotional promise.

The current site retains J9's Canni-Bombs until Jenine sees the alternatives and chooses freely. The discussion should be framed as a creative opportunity, not a correction.
